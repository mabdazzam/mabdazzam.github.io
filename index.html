Muhammad Abdullah Azzam

Hydrologist · Ph.D. researcher at HydroCS Lab, New Mexico State University

Email GitHub Google Scholar LinkedIn CV

I study how water moves across land, rivers, and aquifers, and how models can represent those connections at useful scales. My work brings together hydrologic modeling, scientific computing, and geospatial analysis to examine water availability, drought, and flooding.

At HydroCS Lab, I develop and apply coupled models and open tools, from parallel model workflows to regional and continental data products. Earlier, as a water-resources engineer, I worked on hydraulic and flood-risk studies in Pakistan. Across these projects, I am interested in making modeling methods both useful for water questions and practical to run.

PROJECTS

Current work spans model development, large-scale applications, and hydraulic analysis:

  • VIC–MODFLOW 6 coupling — An MPI-parallel framework for two-way exchange between a land-surface hydrology model and groundwater flow across nonmatching grids. I work on the coupling architecture, numerical verification, reproducibility, and regional applications. The framework and its validation are in progress.
  • Upper Rio Grande and CONUS coupling pilot — Applications of the coupled framework, from a regional basin study to an initial continental-scale run. Current analyses are exploratory, with hydrologic validation continuing.
  • New Mexico drought and water availability — Coupled modeling and climate-scenario analysis for a statewide assessment, documented in NMWRRI Technical Reports 412 and 413.
  • GCN10 — A global Curve Number dataset at 10 m resolution for runoff modeling. The data product is accompanied by a separate C/MPI processing framework and performance work.
  • Hydraulic and flood studies — 1D/2D modeling of the Machar and Hamal Lake plains, compared with satellite-derived inundation maps; climate-scenario flood-risk assessment in the Indus Basin; and hydrologic and hydraulic design studies.
  • Mesilla–Conejos-Médanos aquifer — ParFlow–CLM model development, including regional terrain, geology, wells, and climate-forcing workflows.
  • Large-domain flood modeling — Applying the TORRENT model on HPC systems and improving workflows for computationally demanding inundation simulations.

Other work includes hydrologic tools for GRASS GIS and QGIS, SWAT+ watershed studies, QNSPECT evaluation, and hydrologic time-series analysis. Browse project notes and figures.
