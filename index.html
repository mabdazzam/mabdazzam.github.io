My work connects computational hydrology with hydraulic and flood-inundation modeling. I develop process-based models, run large simulations and data workflows on high-performance computing systems, and use hydrologic and terrain information to study runoff, drought, groundwater, and flood hazards. A recurring thread is connecting large-scale hydrologic simulations with hydraulic flood-risk analysis.

As a Ph.D. researcher in Civil Engineering at New Mexico State University, I build coupled models and open geospatial tools at HydroCS Lab. My background also includes 1D/2D hydraulic studies, flood-risk assessments, and HPC workflow development.

🔬 COMPUTATIONAL HYDROLOGY & FLOOD MODELING

My experience aligns with two connected areas:

  • Computational hydrology: process-based model development and coupling; regional to continental simulations; historical and projected climate forcing; parallel computing and large geospatial data.
  • Hydraulic and flood-inundation modeling: physically based 1D/2D hydraulic analysis, flood mapping and risk assessment, terrain-aware model workflows, and large-domain flood modeling on HPC systems.

1 · Computational hydrology

  • VIC–MODFLOW 6 coupled modeling framework — Developing an MPI-parallel, two-way coupling of VIC and MODFLOW 6, including exchange mapping across nonmatching grids and numerical verification. The framework manuscript is in preparation; regional validation continues. Code
  • Upper Rio Grande groundwater and drought study — Applying the coupled framework to examine water storage and exchange across a regional basin. The current 1980–2025 analysis is exploratory while calibration and scientific review continue.
  • CONUS-scale coupling pilot — A January 2010 run completed 31 daily coupling windows and passed framework-level numerical checks. Hydrologic validation and review of provisional exchange settings are ongoing.
  • New Mexico statewide drought vulnerability — Contributed coupled-model development, climate-data processing, distributed workflows, and water-availability analysis for historical and future climate conditions. Findings and methods appear in NMWRRI Reports 412 and 413.
  • Mesilla–Conejos-Médanos aquifer modeling — Developing ParFlow–CLM inputs and reproducible workflows for terrain, geology, soils, land cover, wells, and climate forcing across a transboundary basin.

2 · Hydraulic and flood-inundation modeling

  • TORRENT flood modeling on HPC systems — Applying a physically based flood model to large domains and improving model and workflow performance for inundation and flood-hazard analysis.
  • Applied hydraulic and flood studies — 1D/2D modeling of the Machar and Hamal Lake plains, checked against Google Earth Engine inundation maps; climate-scenario flood-risk assessment in the Indus Basin; and hydrologic/hydraulic design studies for major water-supply and drainage projects.

At Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, I also benchmarked storage, I/O, compression, and distributed-training workflows across CPU and GPU/APU systems.
