Research focus

Surface water
and groundwater.

At the HydroCS Lab, my research examines how groundwater feedback affects surface-water availability and how to represent those interactions in regional and continental models. I combine numerical modeling, geospatial data, and high-performance computing.

I also study flooding and develop tools for GRASS GIS and QGIS. I participated in the 2025 GRASS GIS Developer Summit.

01 Large-scale computational modeling

Continental hydrologic modeling

An initial CONUS application of the VIC–MODFLOW 6 framework completed 31 daily coupling windows for January 2010. Exchange mapping and domain water-budget checks passed; hydrologic validation and review of provisional exchange settings continue.

02 Model development

VIC–MODFLOW 6 coupling

I develop an MPI-parallel, two-way VIC–MODFLOW 6 coupling framework using the MODFLOW API. The work includes recharge and baseflow exchange, cross-grid mapping, restart and mass-balance checks, and reproducible model runs.

03 Hydraulics and flood modeling

≈

Flood hydraulics
and inundation.

My applied work includes 1D/2D hydraulic modeling for the Manchar–Hamal lake plains, flood-risk assessment in the Indus Basin, and design-flood analysis for water-supply infrastructure. During my internship at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, I also worked on large-domain TORRENT inundation modeling on HPC systems. A manuscript is in preparation.
