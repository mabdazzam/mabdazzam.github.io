Research

My work brings together hydrologic science, computational methods, and open geospatial tools. Current research spans:

  • Hydrologic model development: Coupled surface-water and groundwater models, numerical verification, and regional-to-continental applications
  • High-performance scientific computing: MPI and OpenMP methods, scalable raster processing, and computing workflows for large scientific datasets
  • Geospatial data and open-source GIS: Global runoff products, GRASS GIS development, QGIS plugins, and remote-sensing analysis
  • Water resources and hazards: Groundwater availability, drought assessment, hydraulic modeling, and flood inundation

At the HydroCS Lab, I develop coupled models and evaluate them in New Mexico and continental-scale studies. My work also includes large-domain flood modeling and high-performance computing at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory.

HPC I/O for deep-learning flood prediction

I evaluate how raster-storage formats and data-loader settings affect a flood-prediction workflow across three HPC systems. The study follows performance from CPU reading to single-accelerator and distributed training, examining how hardware architecture and model workload shape end-to-end performance.
