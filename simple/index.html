Research

My current research covers the following topics:

  • Hydrologic model development: Surface–groundwater coupling, numerical verification, regional and continental simulation
  • High-performance scientific computing: MPI/OpenMP, scalable raster processing, and HPC data pipelines for scientific deep-learning workflows
  • Geospatial data and open-source GIS: Global runoff datasets, GRASS GIS add-ons, QGIS plugins, and remote sensing
  • Floods and water availability: Hydraulic and inundation modeling, drought assessment, and regional groundwater studies

At the HydroCS Lab, I build coupled hydrologic models and test them in New Mexico and continental-scale applications. I have also worked on large-domain flood modeling and HPC workflows at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory.

Regional application · analysis in progress

Upper Rio Grande hydrologic modeling

A coupled surface-water and groundwater application across the Upper Rio Grande basin, examining water storage, exchange, and streamflow. The 1980–2025 run is complete; calibration and drought-baseline assessment are ongoing.

A completed January 2010 pilot of daily VIC–MODFLOW 6 exchange across CONUS. The maps demonstrate the continental model setup and run behavior; hydrologic validation continues.
